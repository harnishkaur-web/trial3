The Marking Card

Side 1 of this card is a 30-second routine for every AI answer. Side 2 is a short list of the things you always check.

Why this exists

An AI answer can sound sure and still be wrong.

It can get a date, a number or a name wrong. This card gives you a habit that catches these mistakes before you send the answer.

Your task. Put the four steps of the routine in the right order.

  1. Mark every fact, figure, date, name and source.
  2. Separate what a record supports from what it does not.
  3. Correct anything that is not clearly supported.
  4. Pause before you send the answer.

The card, both sides

This card has a routine and a checklist.

Side 1 shows the routine. Side 2 shows the checklist.

Your task. Tap the card to flip it and read both sides.

Side 1 · The routine

Run it every time. It takes 30 seconds.

1
MarkUnderline every fact, figure, date, name and source in the answer.
2
SeparateDecide if a record supports, cannot confirm or goes against each mark.
3
CorrectVerify, replace, qualify or remove what is not clearly supported.
Pause before send. If you cannot show where a mark came from, do not send it yet.
Side 2 · The checklist

Always check these five things.

FactsClaims stated as true
FiguresNumbers, counts and percentages
DatesDays, months, years and lengths of time
NamesPeople, places and organisations
SourcesWhere the claim says it came from

Side 2, in detail · 1 of 2

Why does each check on side 2 matter?

Each check catches a different kind of mistake. Choose your place, and the examples change to match it.

Your task. Open Facts, Figures and Dates to read why each one matters.

A claim stated as true needs a source before you trust it.
A number is easy to get a little wrong. It is also easy to check.
A vague date like “last Friday” can hide a wrong date.

Names and sources need a check too.

An AI tool can make up a name or a source that sounds real.

The words “according to policy” mean nothing if nobody shows the policy.
